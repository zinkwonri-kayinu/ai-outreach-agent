# Voice & Templates

Treat the templates as models for structure and tone. Don't copy them word for word or fill in their blanks. Every message should read like the sender wrote it for this one person.

## Model 1: problem-first

> Hi Joe, a lot of potential customers may fit your ICP, but knowing which ones have a reason to look is very important.
>
> I can map those buying signals and build a sample list of companies showing them, including the relevant decision-makers and context.
>
> Worth a look?

Structure: an observation about their situation, then the concrete thing I'll build, then a two-to-three-word ask.

## Model 2: offer-first

> Hi Josh,
>
> I'd like to build you a demo system that gets your SDRs better lead lists and higher reply rates for Acme's outbound programs.
>
> I build automations for enrichment, intent signals, and sequencing. I have 50+ clients experience with a background in digital marketing. I can show the system in a short Loom or a quick meeting, whichever works.
>
> Let me know if you would like to know more.
>
> Best,
> Zinkwonri

Structure: the offer, built for their named program, then what I build, then credibility, then a Loom-or-call option, then the ask.

## Hard rules
- Email body **< 80 words**. LinkedIn DM **< 40 words**. Count them.
- **No em dashes (—).** Use a period, comma, or hyphen.
- Use the first name only. Use "Hi <Name>," as the opener. If the first name can't be confirmed, use "Hi," and flag it. Never "I hope this finds you well".
- Name something specific to them: their product, ICP, a role they're hiring, a post, a tool, a competitor.
- Ask about one thing only. Soft asks that fit: "Worth a look?", "Want me to send it over?", "Open to seeing it?", "Should I put it together?"
- Offer delivery as "a short Loom or a quick call, whichever works" when it fits the email. It's usually too long for a DM.
- Credibility line: take it from `context/profile.md`. Rephrase naturally. Use the specific result that matches the prospect's situation.
- Sign off emails with the sender's first name (optionally "Best," before it). DMs can end with just the ask.
- Vary sentence length. Mix one short sentence in with longer ones.
- Plain words: use, simplify, solid, smooth, new, key, dig in, show, market. Avoid leverage, streamline, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, pivotal, transformative, landscape, delve, furthermore.
- Don't use these patterns: myth flip ("Most teams think X..."), rule of three fragments, movie-trailer pairs, "That's not X, it's Y", comma upsells, throat-clear openers, or "Curious what you think?" closers.
- Don't fake familiarity ("Loved your post!"). If you mention a post, reference what it actually said in a few words.
- Don't invent numbers about their business.

## Subject lines (email)
2-5 words, plain, and specific to them. Examples of the style: "signals for <company>", "100 accounts for <company>", "<company> outbound", "quick idea for <first name>". No clickbait, no emojis.
