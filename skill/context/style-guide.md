# Writing style guide: avoid AI slop

Use this as a lens for editing drafts, not a script to fill in. The goal: replace generic, inflated phrasing with specific, concrete detail.

## Patterns to cut

- **Myth flip**: stating a "common belief" then correcting it ("Most teams think X. It's actually Y."). Replace with a real, specific observation instead of a setup/twist.
- **Rule of three**: three short parallel fragments for fake rhythm ("Same standups. Same tickets. Same delays."). Say the specific thing plainly instead.
- **Movie trailer**: two matched sentences where the second twists the first ("Everyone does X. No one does Y."). Just describe what actually happens.
- **The reversal**: rejecting a framing to swap in one grand word ("That's not strategy. It's guessing."). Skip the contrarian pivot; state the finding with a number or fact.
- **The upsell**: inflating the pitch right after a comma ("It's not just a note app, it's a second brain."). State the concrete benefit only.
- **The throat-clear**: filler opener + comment-bait question closer ("In today's fast-paced market... Curious what you think?"). Cut the opener; end on a fact or a real ask, not a generic question.

## Word swaps (AI-flavored → plain)

delve into → dig in · leverage → use · streamline → simplify · robust → solid · seamless → smooth · cutting-edge → new · pivotal → key · transformative → big · tapestry → mix · landscape → market · furthermore → also · shed light on → show

## Formatting tells

- Never use the long em dash (—). Use a hyphen, a comma, or a period.
- Vary sentence length on purpose. Put a short 3-word sentence next to a longer one. AI text tends to write every sentence about the same length; human writing doesn't.

## Applying this to outreach

Swap flagged words, cut the rhetorical patterns above, use real numbers and specifics from the prospect's business instead of generic claims, and vary sentence rhythm so it doesn't read as templated.
